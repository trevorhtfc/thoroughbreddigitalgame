# Newmarket July Course references (owner, 8 Nov 2026)

For building the July Course, the third racecourse: the July Festival of the Newmarket season is run here. Private
reference only (screenshots, maps, photos and TV frames are not ours to publish).

- `aerial-map.jpg`: satellite view of the stands end. The straight runs south-west to the stands; the stands, paddock
  and enclosures sit among mature trees on the near side; pavilions among trees on the far side.
- `enclosure-map.jpg`: the course's own illustrated map of the enclosures: the Garden, Grandstand & Paddock and Premier
  enclosures along the near rail (a series of separate stands), the parade ring and pre-parade rings behind them,
  "Trackside Pavilions" behind the far rail, the winning post opposite the Premier enclosure.
- `stands-empty.jpg`, `stands-raceday.jpg`: the stands: a row of separate low stands, red brick ends, dark timber
  canopy roofs, a red judge's box tower on top, packed lawns and coloured umbrellas between the stands and the rail,
  tall trees behind.
- `undulations-notes-1.jpg`, `-2.jpg`: the ground. The straight mile (the Bunbury Mile) rolls for its first
  three-quarters; after the first two furlongs it runs downhill for about a furlong; then a steep, sustained climb over
  the last two furlongs to the line. Effect: tired horses tie up on the climb (a horse needs to stay the trip), jockeys
  must not kick too early, and the track is fair, wide and galloping.
- `tv-1m5f-frames.jpg`: a frame every 6 seconds of the owner's video of the Bahrain Trophy (1m5f, Racing TV); the
  video itself (a 320 MB screen recording) is not kept in the repo. `tv-1m5f-far-side.jpg`: advertising boards along
  the far rail with the Dyke and trees rising behind. `tv-1m5f-run-in.jpg`: the head-on, the 2f pole, the crowd on the
  stand-side rail, and the finish side-on: a white pavilion and crowd among trees behind the far rail, a long red
  sponsor board along it.
- The owner's references for the stand side's buildings (8 Nov, afternoon): `stands-traditional.png` (plain terraced
  stands with flat dark canopy roofs and red-brick end blocks, a few trees between and behind them), `old-pavilion.png`
  (a two-storey veranda-and-balcony pavilion with fretwork brackets and a picket fence: the stand nearest the line),
  `judges-box-1805.png` (Sartorius's painting of the winning post at Newmarket, 1805: a small brick box with people on its
  roof) and `victorian-house.png` (a red-brick house with steep gables, tall chimneys, a white rail and gate, a bell tent
  and a little hipped pavilion behind). `precedent-pastel-street.png`: the look to follow (pastel tones, soft shadows, a
  smooth clay finish; "don't try too hard to make these look like real buildings"). `owner-note-infill.png`: his note on
  the view back across the line (pavilion taller; marquees and trees to fill the gaps behind the stands).
- `renders/`: stills of the Blender build (tools/blender/build_newmarket_july.py), and `sheet_*.png` of the stand-side
  pieces (tools/blender/build_july_stands.py).

## The owner's brief (8 Nov)

- "A much leafier" course than the Rowley Mile.
- Stand side: a series of grandstands with crowds in and around them; then festival pieces on the stand side down to
  3 to 4 furlongs out.
- Far side: a series of marquees and pavilions nestled in the trees, with a crowd in front; and a tiered structure
  behind the finish.
- The Dyke on the far rail, opposite the grandstands, as at the Rowley Mile.
- The dips and the climb as in his notes, counting in the race as at the Rowley Mile.
- The owner's sketch of both courses (`reference/newmarket/owner-sketch-*.png`, 8 Nov) sets the layout: the July
  straight runs off the Cesarewitch leg at about 25 degrees to the Rowley Mile; its stands are on the side away from the
  Rowley Mile (here the inside of the bend, the horses' right), with a smaller festival area beyond them; the far side
  (towards the Rowley Mile) has the big festival area of marquees in trees, opposite and past the post, with the Dyke
  behind it to about 5f out. The main stand camera looks across the track to the far side.
